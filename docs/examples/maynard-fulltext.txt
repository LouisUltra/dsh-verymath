 Abstract. 

 We introduce a refinement of the GPY sieve method for studying prime k k -tuples and small gaps between primes. This refinement avoids previous limitations of the method, and allows us to show that for each k k , the prime k k -tuples conjecture holds for a positive proportion of admissible k k -tuples. In particular, lim inf n ( p n + m − p n ) < ∞ \liminf_{n}(p_{n+m}-p_{n})<\infty for every integer m m . We also show that lim inf ( p n + 1 − p n ) ≤ 600 \liminf(p_{n+1}-p_{n})\leq 600 , and, if we assume the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture, that lim inf n ( p n + 1 − p n ) ≤ 12 \liminf_{n}(p_{n+1}-p_{n})\leq 12 and lim inf n ( p n + 2 − p n ) ≤ 600 \liminf_{n}(p_{n+2}-p_{n})\leq 600 . 

 1. Introduction 

 We say that a set ℋ = { h 1 , … , h k } \mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{k}\} of distinct non-negative integers is ‘admissible’ if, for every prime p p , there is an integer a p a_{p} such that a p ≢ h ( mod p ) a_{p}\nequiv h\pmod{p} for all h ∈ ℋ h\in\mathcal{H} . We are interested in the following conjecture. 

 Conjecture (Prime k k -tuples conjecture) . 

 Let ℋ = { h 1 , … , h k } \mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\dots,h_{k}\} be admissible. Then there are infinitely many integers n n such that all of n + h 1 n+h_{1} , … \dotsc , n + h k n+h_{k} are prime. 

 When k > 1 k>1 no case of the prime k k -tuples conjecture is currently known. Work on approximations to the prime k k -tuples conjecture has been very successful in showing the existence of small gaps between primes, however. In their celebrated paper [ 5 ] , Goldston, Pintz and Yıldırım introduced a new method for counting tuples of primes, and this allowed them to show that 

 (1.1) 

 lim inf n p n + 1 − p n log ⁡ p n = 0 . \liminf_{n}\frac{p_{n+1}-p_{n}}{\log{p_{n}}}=0. 

 The recent breakthrough of Zhang [ 9 ] managed to extend this work to prove 

 (1.2) 

 lim inf n ( p n + 1 − p n ) ≤ 70 000 000 , \liminf_{n}(p_{n+1}-p_{n})\leq$70\,000\,000$, 

 thereby establishing for the first time the existence of infinitely many bounded gaps between primes. Moreover, it follows from Zhang’s theorem the that number of admissible sets of size 2 contained in [ 1 , x ] 2 [1,x]^{2} which satisfy the prime 2 2 -tuples conjecture is ≫ x 2 \gg x^{2} for large x x . Thus, in this sense, a positive proportion of admissible sets of size 2 satisfy the prime 2-tuples conjecture. The recent polymath project [ 7 ] has succeeded in reducing the bound ( 1.2 ) to 4680, by optimizing Zhang’s arguments and introducing several new refinements.

 The Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem establishes that the primes have level of distribution θ \theta for every θ < 1 / 2 \theta<1/2 , and Elliott and Halberstam [ 1 ] conjectured that this could be extended to every θ < 1 \theta<1 . Friedlander and Granville [ 2 ] have shown that ( 1.3 ) cannot hold with x θ x^{\theta} replaced with x / ( log ⁡ x ) B x/(\log{x})^{B} for any fixed B B , and so the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture is essentially the strongest possible result of this type. 

 The original work of Goldston, Pintz and Yıldırım showed the existence of bounded gaps between primes if ( 1.3 ) holds for some θ > 1 / 2 \theta>1/2 . Moreover, under the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture one had lim inf n ( p n + 1 − p n ) ≤ 16 \liminf_{n}(p_{n+1}-p_{n})\leq 16 . The key breakthrough of Zhang’s work was in establishing that a slightly weakened form of ( 1.3 ) holds for some θ > 1 / 2 \theta>1/2 . 

 If one looks for bounded length intervals containing two or more primes, then the GPY method fails to prove such strong results. Unconditionally we are only able to improve upon the trivial bound from the prime number theorem by a constant factor [ 4 ] , and even assuming the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture, the best available result [ 5 ] is 

 (1.4) 

 lim inf n p n + 2 − p n log ⁡ p n = 0 . \liminf_{n}\frac{p_{n+2}-p_{n}}{\log{p_{n}}}=0. 

 The aim of this paper is to introduce a refinement of the GPY method which removes the barrier of θ = 1 / 2 \theta=1/2 to establishing bounded gaps between primes, and allows us to show the existence of arbitrarily many primes in bounded length intervals. This answers the second and third questions posed in [ 5 ] on extensions of the GPY method (the first having been answered by Zhang’s result). Our new method also has the benefit that it produces numerically superior results to previous approaches.

 2. An improved GPY sieve method 

 We first give an explanation of the key idea behind our new approach. The basic idea of the GPY method is, for a fixed admissible set ℋ = { h 1 , … , h k } \mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{k}\} , to consider the sum 

 (2.1)

 Here χ ℙ \chi_{\mathbb{P}} is the characteristic function of the primes, ρ > 0 \rho>0 and w n w_{n} are non-negative weights. If we can show that S ⁡ ( N , ρ ) > 0 S(N,\rho)>0 then at least one term in the sum over n n must have a positive contribution. By the non-negativity of w n w_{n} , this means that there must be some integer n ∈ [ N , 2 ​ N ] n\in[N,2N] such that at least ⌊ ρ + 1 ⌋ \lfloor\rho+1\rfloor of the n + h i n+h_{i} are prime. (Here ⌊ x ⌋ \lfloor x\rfloor denotes the largest integer less than or equal to x x .) Thus if S ⁡ ( N , ρ ) > 0 S(N,\rho)>0 for all large N N , there are infinitely many integers n n for which at least ⌊ ρ + 1 ⌋ \lfloor\rho+1\rfloor of the n + h i n+h_{i} are prime (and so there are infinitely many bounded length intervals containing ⌊ ρ + 1 ⌋ \lfloor\rho+1\rfloor primes).

 With this choice we find that we just fail to prove the existence of bounded gaps between primes if we assume the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture. The key new idea in the paper of Goldston, Pintz and Yıldırım [ 5 ] was to consider more general sieve weights of the form

As a result we just fail to prove bounded gaps using the fact that the primes have exponent of distribution θ \theta for any θ < 1 / 2 \theta<1/2 , but succeed in doing so if we assume they have level of distribution θ > 1 / 2 \theta>1/2 .

It is the extra flexibility gained by allowing the weights to depend on the divisors of each factor individually which gives this improvement. 

 The idea to use such weights is not entirely new.
